Lesson 1

Comfort kills you slowly.

Comfort beyond a point begins to do more harm than good.

When we enter our 20s, it’s usually the first time in our entire lives that we have freedom. We have more time than ever to think and do what we want (not literally, but you know what I mean). This means we now have a choice on how we can or can not spend our energy and time.

This is good and bad.

This is good because it’s the first step of becoming an adult. At some point, you have to figure out what you want, why you want it, and how to get it. And being in control of one’s life is the first step to making this possible. Being able to make your own choices is incredibly important. And if used correctly, it can set you up for an amazing future.

But for a lot of people, this is bad. Because we can fall into the trap of wanting to avoid discomfort and fall into pure unlimited comfort. This is because we humans naturally try to avoid discomfort at all costs and drown in comfort when we have the choice. And today in the modern world, it’s never been easier to choose to fall into pure comfort. This is what most people do.

And for a while it’s cool! Stay up late. Eat whatever. Chill. Buy stuff. Scroll. Play video games. Do whatever you want. When you’re young, you feel invincible, and you basically are, and you don’t even feel as if you’re doing anything “bad”.

But at some point, if you continue, this flips, and it’s no longer cool, and you’re going to feel empty and unsatisfied.

Part of this is because of the hedonic treadmill.

Which basically means that we are always going back to a baseline level of happiness, no matter what happens.

We can buy this thing, do this, go on this trip, talk to this person, eat this food, scroll scroll scroll, try x y or z, thinking in the moment it’s going to make us happy forever. It doesn’t. It makes us happy for a second or two, not forever. So we try to replace it with more and more stuff, trying to keep ourselves at a certain level of happiness.

We trick ourselves into thinking the next thing we do is FINALLY going to be it, but it never is.

And if we keep trying to reach this ideal “happiness goal”, with vapid, bullshit, cheap dopamine, instant gratification activities that you know aren’t benefiting you long term, it’s going to be a losing battle forever.

For two big reasons:

For one, it’s NEVER going to be enough. Speaking from personal experience. You’re going to keep trying to seek more and more comfort, trying to reach this perfect forever spot that doesn’t exist. You can shower yourself in every comfort, every pleasure for a year, and still end that year feeling as empty as ever. You are NEVER going to be 100% satisfied forever with any singular action.

And two, if you’re always comfortable, 99.6% of the time it means you’re not making progress towards anything useful or that you actually care about on a deeper level. Because the most important things require discomfort, at least in large parts, before you start reaping the rewards towards the end. There aren’t too many things that are worthwhile that come instantly and for free (there might be a couple, but it's not many).

Too much comfort makes you soft. Too much comfort means you're not trying. It means you're not taking a risk; you’re just getting comfortable with mediocrity.

(All of this depends on your life goals. If you want a simpler, chill, more predictable, fun, spontaneous life, then there’s nothing wrong with this at all! It truly depends on what you truly want.)

The older you get, the more you’ll realize that, at some point, comfort is more of a nice-to-have, rather than something you should seek all the time. It’s a bonus to life and a long-term goal. Not something you should shower yourself with constantly, especially this early in life.

I don’t want to sound like those people who say “YoUr 20’S aRe FoR bUIldInG” but your 20’s are the perfect time to build, if that truly is what you want to do with your life.

Now, there’s absolutely nothing wrong with being comfortable inherently. I am not saying you should sleep on a cold floor every night and never do anything nice for yourself. That would be ridiculous. Enjoy the time period you live in; your ancestors would literally kill to be where you stand for one day. But like with anything, too much of it becomes bad.

The dosage makes the poison.

We live in a time in which so much comfort surrounds us; we now have to actively avoid it if we want to achieve more.

(plus also, comfort literally kills you slowly, on a more physical level. If you’re constantly eating badly, not sleeping, and not working out, you’re quite literally dying slowly)

Being too comfortable can slowly creep up on you. Because it doesn’t feel like anything wrong is happening.

First, it’s one day of doing nothing. Then another. Then a week. Maybe you get a burst of motivation and try hard at that thing you want to do for an hour. Then another week passes of doing nothing again. Then a month. Then a season. Then on and on. Then you look back and realize you had an entire year of nothing. Time you’ll never get back.

But your 20’s are the one time in your life when you’re going to have the most energy and leverage to change your life however you want to. So if you waste it all trying to stay comfortable, it’s going to kill you slowly on the inside. You’re going to look back knowing you could’ve made so much more progress towards your goals, but instead you choose to be comfortable and do nothing. And the worst part is that it STILL isn’t enough.

If you do this, you’re going to be in this limbo state of trying to be “happy” but also at the same time wanting to make progress, and be dissatisfied with your life because you’re neither.

The time when I’ve personally made the most progress in my life and my goals is when I was actually the least comfortable. I was either sad, upset, angry, or just not in the best position.

If you’re comfortable, it makes you not want to take action. Because to your brain, it’s reached the state it’s been trying to get to and sees no point in taking more action.

If you’re uncomfortable, you’re going to try to take as much action as possible to stop being uncomfortable and go back to being comfortable.

It’s a constant dance.

But if you trick your brain into thinking you are comfortable by hijacking its reward centers with all the distractions we have surrounding us today, not only will you not be truly comfortable, but you’re also not taking action towards your goals.

And you’re going to keep getting older, then you’re going to look up, realize you did nothing difficult, new, challenging, hard, meaningful.

This is how people “waste their 20s”.

Lesson 2

Time flies, but it’s also incredibly slow.

It’s slow during the day-to-day moments, but fast looking back. Having new experiences, creating meaningful memories, and challenging yourself slows down time.

A lot of people say time flies, and… meh. Yes, but no.

Sure, 5 years feels like it went by in 10 seconds if you sit here right now and try to summarize the past 5 years in 10 seconds. But I personally haven’t been in high school for 7 years now, and it feels like a long 7 years. I haven’t been 15 in over a decade, and it feels like a LONG time since I was 15. It doesn’t feel like years are passing by in minutes as some people say; they’re being a bit dramatic. But again, maybe I haven’t hit that threshold when time really speeds up yet.

A big thing that actually does affect how fast we experience time is how we use it and what we do with it.

And a lot of people feel as if a large part of their youth flies by because we waste a large part of it. This is generally due to distractions and being comfortable, like I mentioned before. And today, this is largely due to doomscrolling, screens, social media addiction, overstimulation, and other modern distractions.

This boils down to time feeling fast because they’re not having new experiences.

A lot of people say time flies by because they’re not doing anything new or different. Which makes sense. Because when something new is happening, your brain is trying to learn as much as it can, so it’s taking its time to learn everything, feeling like time is slowing down.

But if something unimportant or routine happens, your brain has no reason to spend resources trying to remember or soak it in, because it’s already done it in the past.

There’s no new information to slow down and take in.

When you focus heavily on something, that’s when time seems to slow down because even though the same literal time is passing, you’re like a sponge and actually observing and absorbing everything about that moment.

Think about those moments in your life whenever something big, important, or monumental was happening, good or bad. You can probably still remember those moments in good detail even years later. Because your brain noticed that this is important, for whatever reason, and basically was like “lemme take some time to write this down so I can remember it”.

This is why presence is very, very important. Actually, stopping sometimes and soaking in the moments we live, even if they’re mundane. By doing that, you can literally extend your experience of life because you’re actually aware and experiencing more of your life.

Not everyone can decide to turn their life into an adventure, so their brain always has something new to focus on. But you can still get the same effect by simply focusing on the smaller, simpler moments in life and expanding your consciousness into the moment.

Not to sound too woo woo, but it’s true.

Coffee, for example. A lot of people drink coffee daily. But very few people take time to listen to the sound of the beans being ground, smell the air mixed with coffee, the warm feeling on their fingertips, the complexities of the taste as it moves throughout your mouth, etc. But by doing that, they can have 100x more memories and appreciation of that moment rather than just chugging a coffee.

The universe we live in is more complex than you can imagine. Every day we use magic. We are stardust that can speak. We are somehow able to be conscious that we’re conscious. Your brain is more complicated than any machine on earth.

You existing at all is a miracle; there’s always something to soak in and appreciate, no matter where or when you are.

Anyways, there have been times when it feels like I’ve experienced the longest week ever. But then also times when a month would pass, and it feels like it was instant, and I remember almost nothing. Looking back, when things were slower, it was because I was more present and actively engaging in whatever was happening. And I was doing something new or fun or challenging or all the above.

But those fast blurs of time happened when I was being super comfortable, I was in a rut, and did nothing but give into my instant pleasures. Doomscrolling, eating badly, staying up late, I’m not going to repeat myself again lol. The long list of shit we know is bad for us.

These things feel cool in the moment, but don’t form any meaningful memories (At least not when you do them for the 239th time. Eating pizza, staying up late, scrolling is very fun and enjoyable the first couple of times, but like we talked about before, you get into a routine, and it’s no longer new, but just something you do. )

Because of that, when we look back, we see… nothing. And feel like nothing happened. Because there’s nothing memorable to look back on.

This is why doomscrolling is going to rob so many people of a meaningful life. Because it’s the biggest distraction we younger people have by far, it’s getting worse every year, and it’s going to take a huge chunk of our lives and give us memories of nothing but noise if we don’t do something about it.

Also, if you wanna see where your time will go throughout your life, I made a free tool that people loved and say it’s changed the way they view their life. It’s free, and I hope it’s useful to you: https://Malikpeace.com/audit

As you get older and older, you get more and more into a routine; nothing really changes or surprises you anymore. You’re not growing. Your life has plateaued. The new things in life become more and more spaced out instead of happening every day like they used to when we were younger. When we were teenagers, almost every week, there was something new happening. In college, we’re meeting new people, going to events, and just don’t have a care in the world about anything else, making every new day truly new.

And I can easily see how this gets worse and worse over time (time speeding up) as you have less and fewer new experiences. Unless you fixed this with intentional new experiences or presence.

So, if you want time to “slow down” and, in a weird way, “get more life”, experience more life by doing different things and adding more variance and challenge into your life. If someone asks what you've been up to this past year and it's only a sentence, fix that. Make it a goal to make it a paragraph instead.

This is a bonus lesson 2.1.

“You have time” is a myth.

If there is something you want to do, I highly recommend starting it now. It doesn’t mean you need to finish it now, but start it now. The longer you wait to start, the harder and harder it is going to get. I know people in my life who want to do a bunch of different things, but haven’t even started. And now, as they age and are approaching 30+, it's going to be nearly impossible for them to achieve that goal. Not because they can’t, but because the longer you wait, the harder it becomes to start.

Think about it like this: whatever it is that you want to do is like a mountain. When you’re 20, that mountain is 100ft tall. It’s going to be hard to reach the summit no matter what, but if you start when you're 20, when you’re 25, you can be maybe 60 ft up that mountain if you’re focused.

When you’re 30, the mountain is now 500ft tall, you have less time, and you have a 100lb vest on you. This doesn't mean you can't start the climb; it just means it's going to be harder than compared to when you were 20.

As you age, you get more responsibilities. And responsibilities slow you down from doing something different with your life. Of course, this depends on what it is you want to do, but if you have a wife/husband, kids, bills, debt, full-time job, declining health, stress, other commitments, etc. It’s a LOT harder to do something big and make a strong pivot in your life.

Again, I do not want to make it seem like it’s impossible to do something. A lot of older people have been able to make a big change in their lives. But it would be dumb to say it’s not significantly harder and riskier the older you get.

Also, real shit, you could die much sooner than you think.

A lot of us can think, “oh i’m 20, that means I have 50+ years to live” no. There’s the median age IF everything goes well. That means you’re literally a 50% you’re not even going to make it to that age.

You probably know people who have died at a young age. People who’ve gotten cancer and are forever changed. People who lose their limbs who are now limited in what they can do with their lives. If you don’t, I want you to know they exist.

Just because you’re young doesn’t mean you’re going to see what it’s like to be old.

You are here now; the next day is not guaranteed. You have time, yes, but you do NOT have forever. Even if you do have time, it doesn’t matter if you don’t do anything with it.

Time stops for no one. As long as you keep breathing, time will continue to pass. And you WILL reach 30-40-50 one day if you’re lucky enough. Your 20’s are a small window of your life, please use it wisely.

Lesson 3

Put yourself first.

Think for yourself and stop letting others’ opinions influence your entire life.

99.99% of people don’t think about you at all, or at least not nearly as much as you think. They have their own lives they’re living.

When you’re in high school, you think everyone’s always looking at you and cares about your life. When you’re in college, you think the same way. But somewhere between 20 and 24 years old, you slowly start to realize nobody gives a fuck about you because they have other shit to worry about.

And even if someone does think about you, it doesn’t actually tangibly affect your life at all.

And 99% of the time, the .1% of people who really think about you don’t have anything going on within their own life, so they’re going to try to focus on something else other than themselves, which is you. Which still does not affect your life.

If you want to have a simple way of figuring out how much people think about you, ask yourself, how much of your mental energy and time is spent worrying about other people’s lives? Probably very little, if not at all.

And once you realize that people don’t really care and it doesn’t matter if they care about you, it gives you more freedom to live your life on your own terms.

This is important because whenever we are younger, our entire lives and personality are based on the people that we are around. To the point that we care more about what others think about us than what we think about ourselves. This is fine in the beginning as we’re kids, but some people never grow out of this and constantly are worried about what others think of them. This becomes a problem because they never learn who they are when nobody’s opinion is a factor.

Only care about the opinions of the people you care about. Close friends, family, maybe people in your field who might actually tangibly affect your life. But even then, still, put yourself first.

Because you are the only one who is ever going to live your one experience. Meaning you should have the final say on how your existence will go. Live for yourself, not others.

This doesn’t mean be purely egotistical and never ever ever listen to what people say. But it means: listen to what other people say and what they think. Contemplate and incorporate it, but at the end of the day, decide what you want.

What you don’t want to do is look back at the end of your life, realizing you were living for everyone else but yourself. This is one of the biggest regrets people have at the end. (Top 5 Regrets of the Dying by Bonnie Ware)

A lot of people are afraid to think for themselves because they were punished in the past for doing so. And when you’re young, you are so heavily influenced by others around you, you don’t even know who you are until you’re much older. A lot of people don’t find out who they are until they’re 40+, and by then it’s too late, and you’re going to be filled with regret knowing you wasted half your life.

If you’re young, you need to figure out who you are. If you don’t know who you are, create yourself.

If these years are going to dictate the rest of your life, you have to make sure it’s for you.

Just don’t wait too long to figure out who you are, what you think, what your beliefs are.

Remember you, not others. You.

Lesson 4

You are going to suffer no matter what.

So, you might as well get something from it.

When I say suffer, I don’t wanna sound dramatic and act like you’re going to be chained up somewhere getting tortured or something. Nor do I want it to seem like every single second of existence is pure suffering and pain. But that being said –

Everything is suffering.

If you go to the gym, it hurts, and you’re suffering.

If you don’t, you get unhealthy, and you’re suffering.

If you don’t take a risk you’re suffering knowing you could’ve but didn’t.

If you are taking a risk, you’re suffering from doubting yourself and dealing with uncertainty.

If you’re on your phone 24/7 doing nothing, you’re suffering.

If you’re grinding or really in the trenches, you’re suffering.

There is nothing I can think of in which suffering isn’t attached in some way.

Even with drugs, which are supposed to make you feel 100% bliss and no suffering, will make you suffer at some point.

You can never fully 100% avoid it.

Even if you somehow avoid suffering for a time, you’re going to adapt to the removal of suffering and suffer again. Because your brain is constantly looking for a problem to solve, even when things seem like they’re good. And once it finds a problem, this is going to cause suffering again, making it a constant of the human condition.

Think about it like this. Imagine you were given every single thing you want right this second. A house, family, freedom, health, whatever. Imagine you were there for a week, a month, or a year. If you sit with that thought for a minute, you can probably already start to find problems with that situation. Showing that even if you were given everything, you would still find a way to suffer.

Now I don’t see this as a bad thing per se, instead I see it almost like an equation.

If suffering is going to happen no matter what, then I can expect it and ignore it. I can simply just look at the end result of what I want and then go get it without expecting a clean smooth path, because I know that no matter what, suffering is the cost.

And also, when I am suffering, I don’t make myself feel bad about it.

Sometimes when suffering, we can feel bad about suffering, which leads to more suffering. Then we suffer more suffering because we feel bad about suffering suffering and it’s this non-stop suffering loop.

Simply just accepting it as a part of life and moving towards any way, is way more useful than trying to solve this impossible equation that’s part of being human.

And I’ve been saying this for years: No matter what path you take in life, it’s going to be hard. Why not take the path that gives you the most at the end?

Lesson 5

Think. Think. Think about your life.

This is probably the most Important thing here, honestly. At least the top 3.

Most people don’t think.

Of course, they technically do, but not really on a deeper level. Especially for younger people in their 20s. We just don’t take any real time to think. Even though it’s THE most important time to think, ask questions, and figure out stuff, because depending on how you think will dictate whether or not your life moves up, down, left, right, or in circles.

In my experience, most younger people don’t because we’re constantly distracted by our phones. When I was younger, it was sleep, school, sports, work, video games, and a lot of phone in the middle to fill the gaps. Almost zero time went to actually thinking about my life alone for hours with no other forms of stimulation to distract me, but I think it’s one of the most important things I’ve ever done in my entire life.

Which is fucking crazy if you think about it. Think about your age right now, then multiply it by 8765. That’s how many hours there are in a year. This will tell you how many hours of your life you've lived so far. Then ask yourself, how many of those hours have been spent sitting alone with no other forms of stimulation, so you can think about your life?

If you’re 20 right now that means you’ve been alive for 175,300 hours. Let’s say you’ve only thought about your life for a total of 3 hours over the course of your life, which honestly might be high for some people. That means you’ve spent 0.0017% of your life actually thinking about your life.

Which is absolutely frightening, the first time I thought about it like this.

Because when you think about your life, you’re examining it. And again, your brain looks for problems in everything and tries to solve those problems. So, when you look at your life, you’re bringing all the problems of your life on full display, giving you the chance to solve them.

But it’s one of the most painful things you can do because it’s your life. It's different if we're looking at something we can turn away from, but we can't turn away from ourselves. We have to confront our lives.

This is NOT easy. It’s very simple to just say “think about your life,” but it’s one of the most uncomfortable things any human can do, but necessary if you wanna get the most out of your existence. You can never expect to fix or change your life if you don’t do this.

Sit down and just think.

Figure out what YOU want.

Why do you want what you want?

How are you going to get what you want?

Do you ACTUALLY care about what you think you want?

What do you find meaningful?

Why do you find it meaningful?

Do you think life has meaning?

Is everything meaningless?

Do you believe there’s a God or no God?

What does that mean to you?

What goals do you have for yourself?

Why are you pursuing them?

If you keep pursuing them, where will it lead you?

Do you want the end result of what you’re pursuing?

What have you done with your life so far?

Have you achieved anything?

Are you happy? Are you sad? Are you fulfilled? Why? Why not?

Why? Why anything?

Who are you?

And on and on and on.

These questions are very, very uncomfortable, but if you run from them, you’ll run from your life.

Even outside of just deep thoughts of your life, think about everything. Ask questions. Question everything. Think about your decisions beyond the immediate consequences of them.

Become genuinely interested in existence itself. Remove distractions every now and then and just ponder. You get to be here only once, and you form so much more in-depth thinking when you stop and let your brain off the leash and just let it explore.

Now you can get lost in your mind, so make sure you’re still doing stuff and not just thinking 24/7. But most people don’t even think deeper about their lives at all, outside of a passing thought, which is scary.

Use your brain, it’s the most complex thing in the universe.

Lesson 6

Drinking is overrated. In my opinion, of course.

You feel tired and awake at the same time and are more likely to react on your impulses.

(This isn’t to put down anyone who drinks, not at all. Just my personal opinions and experience so far.)

I’m one of the rare people who never drank before 21. I actually didn’t really try to drink until I was 22, I think. And I tried drinking because I was like, “Hey, I’m finally old enough to drink, let’s do it!”And it’s fine, I guess?

It does feel nice because you feel relaxed and you don’t care about much. But you start to lose control. I never got super super drunk, but I can easily see how it can get bad very quickly because your judgement just takes a shit the more you drink.

The best I can describe it, is to imagine the thoughts you have right now. You have 1000s. But there’s a little wall that’s stopping you from acting on and saying the thoughts in your mind. But as you drink, that wall gets lower and lower, and now suddenly every little thought and first action just happens because nothing is stopping it.

This is when people typically warn you about drinking. Not that drinking itself is bad, but that too much of it can lead to problems.

I am still incredibly new to drinking. I know there are different types of alcohol as well. I am 100% going to experience more as I get older. But I say it’s overrated, because I thought it was going to be some magical experience.

But again, I’ve only drank maybe twice, so take what I say as a very small grain of salt, and do whatever you want, just stay safe.

Lesson 7

95% of things do not matter nearly as much as you think.

You probably don’t remember anything you were worried about 5 years ago, and you won’t remember what you’re worried about now 5 years from now.

A quote I like a lot goes “I have known many a great troubles, most of which never happened”

This has been an ever-increasing true statement in my life.

I can’t say exactly what happened, but I was 19-20 when something happened. I don’t remember 100% what it was, but I know it had a lot of emotional buildup. I remember it being this thing I was worried about for a long time. Then the thing finally happened. And I remember right after, I stopped, stared off for a second, and just thought, “huh… That shit didn’t matter, did it?”

The fact that I can’t even tell you what that situation means shows that it truly didn’t matter.

Over time, this has become a larger part of my reality. Most things don’t matter.

Not saying NOTHING matters and there’s nothing to take seriously to put your focus on. But most things don’t and aren’t worth putting much attention on. Not only things like other people, politics, news and other things you can’t control, I even mean smaller things.

Let’s say you lose your keys, for example. You could sit there and freak out and worry about it, or just stay calm and just find your keys. You are going to find your keys again within due time. And if stressing out about it isn’t going to help you find them, there’s simply no point in doing so.

You can freak out and worry about anything if you choose to. But if it’s not going to make a difference in the situation, there’s no point. This doesn’t mean you’re going to situations when your brain is just going to freak out and stress; that’s normal. But 95% of situations, you are making the conscious choice to continue worrying about something that won’t matter 5 minutes later.

Also, worrying about everything takes away energy you could put towards more useful things.

I read Subtle Art of Not Giving a Fuck by Mark Manson years ago; I should probably read it again.

But the biggest takeaway I got is: if you care about everything, you actually care about nothing. You only have a certain amount of “cares” or “fucks” to give. And if you give everything the same amount of attention, you’re telling yourself everything matters to you equally. And you remove how much care can be put on the things you actually care about.

But if you sit down and actually think (remember the other lesson) about what you ACTUALLY care about and what ACTUALLY matters to you, you can remove the wasteful energy you’re putting into other things, and redirect it towards what you care about most. Please, please, please, please. DO NOT lose your fire. Do not lose your fight. Do not give up on your past dreams.

You are going to get there one day. You life right now is built on the choices of your past self. And your future self is going to be built on the choices you make today.

Take some time to think about yourself, older and wiser. Think about where you want to be. What you want to achieve in the future. Think about the feeling of pride and joy knowing you stuck with your goals and habits till the end, even through the toughest parts.

Think about what they would think about you right now. Are they proud of your actions? Do they appreciate the sacrifices your making for them?

If they were to walk into the room with you right now, what would they tell you to do? Would they tell you to give up? Stop? To be more realistic? To try less and just give in?

When you get older, ask yourself, what would they want? Who do they want to become?

Who are you going to become?

Whenever I think about my past and future self, I get emotional. Because it’s not some abstract idea like a lot of things are, but real. I actually was that kid. I actually am going to get older. The same thing happens with every single person we see around us.

As you continue to age, you are going to see the web on your life and the choices you make. You can only see it looking back, but visualize it moving forward. And the best way to figure out what to do moving forward is to figure out what the web would make you most proud looking back. Not what web you think others would like, but a web you want.

By doing this, it makes it much easier to make decisions and decide what to do. Not only are the actions now clearer, but also more fulfilling.

Think about your older self. Where are they living? What they have accomplished. What they’ve been through. What principles do they live by? Sit down, understand it, feel it, then take as much action as possible to make them proud and turn yourself into them.

Use your younger self’s energy and dreams as fuel. Use your older self’s memories as a compass.

I mentioned this before, but I want to really drill this in one more time. You are the only you that is ever going to exist. You are the only one who is going to live your life from beginning to end this one time. So you need to make damn sure you’re at least living for you.

You do not want to reach the end of your one life, realizing you let everyone else dictate the direction of your life.

And the best people to live for and figure out what to do are who you were in the past and who you’re going to be in the future.

Do not let yourself down. You are all you’re going to have from beginning to end.

Bonus

Learn about Money

This is the final lesson I want to leave you with.

Money is important. Duh.

But when you’re young, you don’t really think about money much and just kinda expect it to be a big thing but don’t really realize truly how important it is.

Money, past the age of like 25, becomes the #1 thing on your mind and will dictate what you do with your time. It will control where you’ll live. The experiences you’ll have. It controls every single aspect more than almost any single other category of life by far.

Money can either make your life heaven or hell. Money dictates your freedom and controls your entire life.

It’s not something you should forget about until the time comes to worry about it, because if you do, it alone has the potential to fuck up your life.

Now I’m not going to go into crazy detail about money here because it will take forever lol.

But I will explain a couple of bullet points and leave you to go much deeper into it.

To make this very simple. Money = Value.

Society is basically just other people helping other people with stuff. But people aren’t going to do anything for free. They need something on value in return, if they are going to provide value to you.

In the past, we would trade things with people as individuals, known as the barter system. But this didn’t work long-term because, at some point, people didn’t find the things being traded as valuable. So we invented money. A universal exchange of value. So now, when someone does something for someone, they get value in the form of money. And because we all agree that money as value, the whole system works.

And when you have a lot of money, you can give it to others, and they can do things that make your life easier. They can build you a house. Build a car. Pump oil from the ground. Make your food. Give you entertainment, etc. All because you are giving them value back in return in the form of money.

You can not exist alone. If you want to free up your time, you need to have money to give to other people, so you don’t have to spend your time doing things you don’t want to do. If you had to truly live alone, you wouldn’t have time to do literally 1/1,000,000th of the things needed to have the life you have now. (You’d have to make the internet, the house you live in, manage food, etc. All by yourself.)

Most people don’t have millions in the bank, so they need to work for money. They have to give up the most precious resource they have (their time) so they can get money in return for it. This is perfectly fine in the beginning, almost everyone does it, but it’s not a long term goal.

Because if you have to work for money the rest of your life, you’re never going to be truly free of your time. If you never have enough money to stop working, you’ll have to keep working just to survive.

The only way to have true time freedom is to make enough money that you don’t have to worry about trading your time for money anymore.

Some people do this in multiple ways. The most popular is retirement. The idea that you’re going to save and invest money over a long period of time and it’s going to compound into a large amount of money you can live off of for the rest of your life.

This is where thinking about money and taking time to think and do math can become very very important.

If you sit down and do the math, you’re going to realize you’re going to be working for the rest of your life until you’re 60 before you can have freedom, the best-case scenario if everything works perfectly.

But life isn’t perfect, so long-term, this probably isn’t going to work. It can work in theory, but the reality is much different.

I explain much more in a video called “”young people just don’t want to work anymore””

If you want to have true freedom, I recommend really really thinking about how you’re going to make a large amount of money, especially while you’re younger. Not 100 million dollars in 2 months. Not get rich quick. But actually thinking about how you’re going to make a sizable amount of income without spending 50 years working for it.

In my opinion, the only way to do this is to be a part of or own a successful business while you’re young. Because everything at the end of a day is a business, if you work a job, you’re working in a business. You’re just a small part of it. And the way you make lots of money is by becoming more important in an important business.

So you need to make yourself more valuable and figure out what you can offer your employer or customers that’s useful and valuable.

If you’re going to start a business, this is something to always remember.

Money = Value

A lot of money = Value x Scale

If you can provide a lot of value to people at scale, you’re going to be rich. And if done right you can set yourself up for true freedom and fulfillment while helping others.

Now, all of this goes extremely deep and is something that never truly ends.

But I highly recommend, especially while you’re young, read books, learn, listen, talk about money so you actually understand it and start taking the right steps to set your future up correctly.

You don’t need to start a business. Some people will have you believe you need to be a CEO in your 20’s to make money. You don’t. But you do need to be valuable and take the right actions.

A lot of people don’t do this. They go to college at 18, not knowing what they’re doing. Get a degree they don’t care about and isn’t valuable. Get in 10’s of thousands of dollars in debt and set themselves up to be ruled by a dollar their entire lives.

This is not everyone but it’s a lot.

Please, if you’ve made it this far, take time to learn about money and business and make yourself valuable or build something so you’re able to have control and leverage over your life.

Money is a tool. Use it wisely.

I know this wasn’t in-depth, but I hope it’s a useful start. This is one of the most important lessons you can learn, especially while you’re young.
